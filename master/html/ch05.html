The chapter on GTFS data builds the trips that the Brussels public transport company STIB-MIVB plans to run. This chapter loads the trips its vehicles actually ran: the positions of every bus, tram and metro, recorded every 20 seconds. It builds one trajectory per vehicle, explores a day of service, detects vehicles that stopped or crawled between stops, follows the vehicles live, and serves their trajectories as vector tiles for a map.

The data comes from MobilityTwin.Brussels, a platform of the CoDE laboratory of the Université libre de Bruxelles that collects the open mobility data of Brussels. It polls the real-time API of STIB-MIVB every 20 seconds, follows each vehicle from poll to poll, and matches it to the GTFS trip it is running. The platform keeps the positions of the recent months, from 29 September 2026 onwards, as one Parquet file per day, and the GTFS timetable that was valid on each day. Its documentation asks the users of the data to cite the paper MobilityTwin.Brussels by Gaspard Merten and Mahmoud Sakr (SIGSPATIAL 2023).

Contents

This module covers the following topics:

  • Downloading a day of vehicle positions and its GTFS timetable

  • Building one trajectory per vehicle, with gaps, together with temporal attributes: the GTFS trip, the delay, and the status at a stop

  • Computing commercial speeds and delays per line

  • Detecting vehicles that stopped or crawled between stops

  • Appending the positions of the live feed to the trajectories

  • Serving the trajectories as Mapbox vector tiles
